About

Where machine learning meets public health

Gerald Munetsi is a researcher working at the intersection of deep learning and infectious-disease modelling. He is a Mastercard Foundation Scholar and has completed an MPhil in Population Health Sciences at the MRC Biostatistics Unit, University of Cambridge, having previously earned a first degree in Applied Mathematics from the University of Zimbabwe.

He is currently an Assistant Research Fellow at the Centre for Epidemiology Modelling and Analysis (CEMA), where he contributes to the following projects: Dynamic Transmission Modelling for Schistosomiasis and Soil-Transmitted Helminths for the WHO Expanded Project for Elimination of Neglected Tropical Diseases (NTDs), and Mathematical Modelling to Establish the Transmission Dynamics and Burden of Visceral Leishmaniasis in Kenya and Ethiopia to Inform Targeted Control and Elimination Strategies (ENDVL Afrika).He is also currently implementing a Policy Fellowship awarded by the Africa Pandemic Sciences Collaborative, focusing on AI-enhanced One Health surveillance.

His research interests centre on:

  • Infectious-disease and NTD modelling using compartmental and agent-based models
  • Surrogate modelling and approximate Bayesian inference
  • Geostatistical modelling
  • Scalable and flexible methods for spatiotemporal statistics and Bayesian machine learning

Beyond his technical work, Gerald has collaborated with several NGOs across Zimbabwe, the UK, and South Africa. His experience includes leading youth development and outreach initiatives, writing grant proposals, and engaging stakeholders to support impactful social programmes.
